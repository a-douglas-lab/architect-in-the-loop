## What the runs showed

- **Run 3 caught a factual error in the author's own advice** (High 1): Andy had been told Claude Code has no allowlist setting, and he decided D1 on that basis. The setting, `permissions.blockReadsOutsideWorkingDirectories`, was in the documentation the author had fetched. This is the strongest evidence so far that the reviewer is worth its cost. It also shows that the author's research can't be trusted unchecked, even on a page it has read.
- **Run 4's Mediums were mostly contradictions between decided rules,** not writing errors. Written out as separate constraints, E2's text conflicted with P2 (publication), with D6 (who builds drivers before the capability exists) and with itself (green-after for tests nothing may pass). As in run 2, an exact transcription of an ambiguous decision exposes the ambiguity; only a ruling fixes it.
- **Defaults in the front matter are decisions too.** Tier and exceptions had no source in either batch. ADR-000@v3 C13 now sets a default, so the reviewer doesn't have to raise the same finding every batch.
